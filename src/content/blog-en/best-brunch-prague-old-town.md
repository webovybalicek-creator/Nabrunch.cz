<p>The streets around Old Town Square and Charles Bridge are full of cafés – but many of them serve overpriced, tired breakfasts to tourists who will never come back. The good news: a few minutes' walk away, there are small cafés and bakeries where <strong>Praguers actually go for breakfast</strong>. We have visited all of them ourselves. Here are our favourites in the historic centre.</p>

<h2>Old Town (Staré Město)</h2>

<h3>👉🏼 <a href="/en/places/la-forme-rytirska-praha/">La Forme</a> – Rytířská street</h3>
<p>One of Prague's best-rated bakeries, three minutes from Old Town Square. Filled croissants, danishes and sweet swirls baked on site, with specialty coffee or matcha. <strong>Opens at 7:30 on weekdays</strong> – ideal before the crowds arrive.</p>
<p>🥐 <strong>What to order:</strong> a filled croissant and a flat white. Come early – pastries sell out.</p>

<h2>New Town (Nové Město)</h2>

<h3>👉🏼 <a href="/en/places/coffee-bird-praha/">Coffee Bird</a> – near the National Theatre</h3>
<p>A tiny breakfast café that feels like a friend's living room, next to the Globe bookstore. Egg dishes, homemade granola, Czech semolina porridge with chocolate and French toast with blueberry compote. Breakfast is served until 14:00, every day.</p>
<p>🍳 <strong>What to order:</strong> semolina porridge with chocolate – a Czech childhood classic.</p>

<h3>👉🏼 <a href="/en/places/kavarna-muna-praha/">Muna</a> – Myslíkova street</h3>
<p>An Argentine café famous for <strong>medialunas</strong> – softer, slightly sweet cousins of the croissant, including pistachio and almond versions. Specialty coffee, outdoor seating, dogs welcome. Open daily from 8:00.</p>
<p>🥐 <strong>What to order:</strong> one classic and one pistachio medialuna.</p>

<h2>Lesser Town (Malá Strana)</h2>

<h3>👉🏼 <a href="/en/places/stranou-praha/">Stranou</a> – Hellichova street</h3>
<p>A cosy vaulted bistro ten minutes from Charles Bridge, right by the Petřín funicular. Seasonal breakfast menu from 8:00 to 15:00: creamy scrambled eggs with pumpkin crunch, a Czech-style bistro plate with ham and hermelín cheese, or plum pancakes with gingerbread crumble.</p>
<p>🍳 <strong>What to order:</strong> the plum pancakes – then walk up Petřín Hill.</p>

<h2>Tips for breakfast in central Prague</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Go early.</strong> Most good cafés open between 7:30 and 8:30. By 10:00 at weekends, popular places fill up.</li>
<li><strong>Walk five minutes away from the main squares.</strong> Prices drop and quality goes up.</li>
<li><strong>Cards are accepted almost everywhere.</strong> Tipping around 10 % is usual for table service.</li>
<li><strong>Check opening hours on Czech public holidays</strong> – many small cafés close or shorten their hours.</li>
</ul>
